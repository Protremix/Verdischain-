## 1. Token Classification

### Current Assessment
Based on the utility-first design of VERDIS:
- VERDIS is primarily designed as a **utility token** for transaction fees, smart contract execution, staking, and ecosystem services
- The token does NOT represent ownership, equity, or debt
- No dividends or profit-sharing mechanisms exist
- Staking rewards are inflation-based (network emission), not profit-based

### Risk Factors
- The Howey Test (US) and similar frameworks in other jurisdictions may classify tokens as securities depending on:
  - Marketing language used
  - Expectation of profit
  - Common enterprise structure
  - Efforts of others (promoter/developer team)
- Even utility tokens can be classified as securities if marketed with investment expectations

### Required Legal Review
- **Jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction token classification analysis** by qualified securities lawyers
- **Securities law compliance** for each target jurisdiction
- **Utility token legal opinion** from qualified counsel
- **Marketing compliance review** to ensure no investment-return promises

## 2. Public Offering Rules

### Presale / IDO
- Public token sales may trigger:
  - US: Securities Act of 1933 registration requirements (unless exempt)
  - EU: Prospectus Regulation (EU 2017/1129)
  - UK: Financial Services and Markets Act 2000
  - Singapore: Securities and Futures Act
  - Other jurisdictions: Local securities regulations

### Potential Exemptions
- **Reg D (Rule 506(c))**: Accredited investors only, with verification
- **Reg S**: Offshore offering to non-US persons
- **Reg A+**: Mini-IPO with SEC qualification
- **EU Private Placement**: Limited to qualified investors
- Each exemption has specific requirements, limitations, and filing obligations

### Required Legal Review
- Determine applicable exemption strategy for each jurisdiction
- Prepare offering documents compliant with chosen exemptions
- File required forms with relevant regulators
- Implement geographic restrictions

## 4. KYC / AML Requirements

### Required Implementation

- **Anti-Money Laundering (AML)**: Transaction monitoring
  - Source of funds verification for large purchases
  - Suspicious activity reporting
  - Transaction monitoring thresholds
  - Record-keeping (5+ years)

- **Sanctions Screening**:
  - OFAC sanctioned individuals/entities check
  - UN, EU, UK sanctions list screening
  - PEP (Politically Exposed Persons) screening
  - Adverse media screening

### Required Legal Review

- Selection of KYC/AML service provider

- Record-keeping requirements per jurisdiction

## 6. Marketing Restrictions

### Prohibited Marketing Practices
- **Guaranteed returns**: No promises of profit or investment returns
- **Future value predictions**: No statements about expected token price
- **Investment advice**: Token is not an investment vehicle
- **FOMO tactics**: No artificial scarcity claims or urgency manipulation
- **Misleading comparisons**: No comparisons implying guaranteed performance

### Required Disclosures in All Marketing
- "This is not an investment. VERDIS is a utility token for the Verdis ecosystem."
- "Token prices may fluctuate. There is no guarantee of token value."
- "Participants may lose the full amount spent on tokens."
- "This document is not investment advice."
- Geographic restriction notice

### Required Legal Review
- Marketing material review by legal counsel
- Social media policy compliance
- Influencer marketing guidelines
- White-paper legal review

## 11. Data Protection (GDPR / CCPA)

### GDPR Compliance (EU)
- Personal data collected during KYC must be:
  - Processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently
  - Collected for specified, explicit purposes
  - Adequate, relevant, and limited
  - Accurate and kept up to date
  - Retained no longer than necessary
  - Processed securely with appropriate safeguards

- Data Subject Rights:
  - Right of access
  - Right to rectification
  - Right to erasure
  - Right to restrict processing
  - Right to data portability
  - Right to object

### CCPA Compliance (California)
- Privacy policy disclosure
- Right to know what personal information is collected
- Right to delete personal information
- Right to opt-out of sale of personal information
- Non-discrimination for exercising rights

### Required Legal Review
- Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)
- Privacy policy drafting
- Data processing agreements with vendors
- Cross-border data transfer mechanisms
- Data breach response plan
